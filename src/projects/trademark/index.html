a 3d-printed recreation of chamber's trademark from valorant — a stationary trip designed to detect people and trigger when someone walks into it. the build combines a multi-part printed assembly, a hollow head for the electronics, and custom hardware running on an esp32-cam.

inspiration for the project

i play a lot of valorant, and recently came across videos of people making raze's boombot and killjoy's turrent from valorant, so i wanted to do something similar. i figured chamber's trademark would be a fun idea, so i decided to use that and turn it into an ai security camera of some sorts.

3d modeling process

everything was modeled in fusion 360. modeled mostly by feel and looks rather than dimensions.

the top half is built around a circular base with a back section that holds the "head," which is where the camera lives. the neck slots onto the base with enough clearance to rotate, and the head still needs a proper connection to the back — a snap fit is the plan, but that's one of the parts i'm still working out. the sphere was also hollowed out so an acrylic piece, the pcb, and a potential battery can fit inside.

the printed base and neck fit together correctly, and the head only needed a small extrusion fix before it printed properly. the whole thing has been sanded and prepared for paint, but the color scheme is still on hold.

electronics

the electronics plan uses an esp32-cam, a pir sensor, ir leds, and mosfets. i started the pcb early by building a schematic and a component library in fusion 360, partly so the board layout could inform the enclosure dimensions.

the first esp32-cam came from miles, but i shorted its voltage regulator while trying to get it connected — so that one died before it ever made it into the build. while waiting on replacements and the rest of the parts, i tested the wireless side with a spare esp32: a button press posts to a small flask server on a laptop, which closes any open firefox windows and opens a new tab. the esp32-to-laptop connection worked, which proved out the network part of the trigger chain.

current plans / to be added

  • finish the pcb — the board is still mostly a schematic and component library until the remaining parts arrive
  • paint the enclosure — sanding is done, but the final color scheme still needs to be sprayed
  • mount the acrylic and battery — the hollowed sphere has room for the lens piece, board, and a potential battery
  • finish the head connection — work out a printable snap fit, and decide whether the head should rotate
  • get detection working — wire up the pir sensor and ir leds so the trap can actually detect and trigger
  • assemble and test — fit everything into the printed housing and run the full system end to end
